1st place · Future Mobility Challenge 2023

Traffic Sign Classification for Autonomous Vehicles

Deep convolutional networks trained to read 43 classes of traffic sign from 32×32 pixel images — built from scratch, then beaten by transfer learning from VGG16 and ResNet V2.

1st place — $500 prize
Mentor
Ryan Ahmed, PhD, MBA — McMaster University
Stack

Dataset
34,799 train · 4,410 val · 12,630 test

The challenge

The automotive industry is shifting from human-driven vehicles to autonomous, AI-powered ones. Self-driving systems are expected to save hundreds of thousands of lives and create an economic opportunity measured in trillions by 2035 — but only if perception is reliable. Reading a stop sign correctly, every time, in rain and glare, is the unglamorous core of that.

The task: train and evaluate several deep CNNs to classify traffic-sign images. Colour images at 32×32 pixels, 43 classes, split into 34,799 training, 4,410 validation and 12,630 held-out test images the model never sees during training.

What I built

  1. Researched the AlexNet architecture and mapped each layer's job: convolution and feature extraction, max pooling, flattening, dense classification.
  2. Visualised a sample image from every class with its label, as a sanity check on the data.
  3. Trained an AlexNet-style CNN over 15 epochs, plotting accuracy against epoch to see where it plateaued.
  4. Evaluated on the holdout test set — data the network had never touched.
  5. Fine-tuned on additional images collected from the web and from a phone camera.
  6. Ran three separate accuracy experiments: more epochs, extra convolution and pooling layers, and dropout regularisation.
  7. Built three further architectures varying kernel counts, dense-layer width, activations and regularisation, and compared them.
  8. Tested the best networks on brand-new signs photographed outside the dataset.

What made the difference

  • Transfer learning beat scratch training. Fine-tuning VGG16 and ResNet V2 got more out of 34k small images than any network I trained from zero.
  • Dropout mattered more than depth. Adding layers past a point bought training accuracy and lost test accuracy; dropout closed the gap.
  • The holdout set is the only honest number. Validation accuracy drifted optimistic as soon as I started tuning against it.

The result

First place out of the competing teams, and a $500 prize.
