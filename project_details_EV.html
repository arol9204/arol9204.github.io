EV Adoption in Washington State

A Tableau story tracking how fast the electric fleet is growing, which manufacturer owns which city, and how range, price and charging speed actually compare once you put them side by side.

Prospective EV buyers & policy readers

Why this data

US Department of Energy investment in battery development cut battery cost by roughly half, and within four years genuinely affordable EVs reached the market. Buyers now choose between hybrids, plug-in hybrids and full battery-electric vehicles. Washington State publishes a registration-level dataset of every one of them — which makes it the right place to ask what adoption actually looks like on the ground.

Questions the story answers

1. How fast is the EV fleet growing?

  • Which manufacturers and models sell best?
  • Which manufacturer dominates each city in the state?
  • What separates all-electric from plug-in hybrid buyers, by manufacturer and by city?
  • Which brands commit to all-electric, which hedge with hybrids, and which do both?
  • Which cities hold the highest concentration of electric vehicles?

2. How do the vehicles themselves compare?

  • Electric range across manufacturers.
  • Speed and performance across manufacturers.
  • Fast-charging rate across manufacturers.
  • Retail price against efficiency — the trade-off buyers actually face.

3. What should a buyer do with this?

The final dashboard is built as a decision aid: filter to a budget and a minimum range, and see which models survive.

Vehicle comparison dashboard

Range, performance, charging rate and price on one canvas, so no single spec gets to tell the whole story on its own.

What I took from it

  • A story beats a dashboard when the audience does not already know the question — sequence carries more than interactivity.
  • Spider charts earn their keep for multi-spec comparison, and almost nothing else.
  • Registration data is a proxy for adoption, not adoption itself; saying so in the viz is part of the analysis.
