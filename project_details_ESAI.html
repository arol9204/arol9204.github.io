Why I built it

I moved to Canada as a non-native English speaker and wanted to get better at speaking. Language courses that give real feedback are expensive; free practice gives you no feedback at all. So I built the missing piece: something that listens to an answer and tells you honestly how it landed.

What it does

  1. The app poses an exam-style question, of the kind used in IELTS and CELPIP speaking sections.
  2. The user records a spoken answer.
  3. Whisper transcribes the audio.
  4. The LLM receives both the question and the transcript, so it can judge whether the answer is actually on topic.
  5. It returns an assessment covering grammar, vocabulary usage, coherence and fluency.

Passing the question alongside the transcript is the detail that makes the feedback useful. Scoring a transcript alone tells you the English was fine; scoring it in context tells you the answer missed the point.

How I built it

Development started in Google Colab, where I wired OpenAI's Whisper model to a language model through LangChain and put a Gradio interface on top so it could be used by anyone with a browser and a microphone.

Demo

What I took from it

  • Transcription quality sets the ceiling on everything downstream — a mis-heard word reads as a grammar error.
  • The prompt is part of the product. Asking for rubric-shaped output made the feedback consistent enough to act on.
  • Building the thing I needed myself was a far better spec than any requirement list.
